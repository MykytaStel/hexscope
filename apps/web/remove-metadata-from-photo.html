How to remove EXIF and metadata from a photo

A photo carries more than its picture: where it was taken, the camera and its serial number, the time to the second, the program that edited it. hexscope removes all of it and keeps the picture exactly as it was, in your browser. The photo is not uploaded anywhere.

1 What a photo carries

  • EXIF: the location, the date and time with the time zone, the camera and lens, their serial numbers, the owner's name and the software.
  • XMP: Adobe's block, which often repeats the location and the dates, and keeps a history of the edits.
  • IPTC: the caption, keywords, credits and the place in words.
  • The maker's notes: more serial numbers, and on an iPhone the IDs that tie a photo to its Live Photo and the rest of its burst.
  • A thumbnail: a small copy of the picture, which an editor that crops the photo may leave uncropped.
  • Content Credentials and AI prompts: who or what made the picture, and the prompt it was made from.

Every field, with its value and where it lies in the file: EXIF viewer: what a photo's metadata says.

2 Remove it from one photo

Open the photo in hexscope. It reads JPEG, an iPhone's HEIC, PNG, WebP, AVIF and TIFF. Under What this photo reveals, press Remove it — save a clean copy. In the copy:

  • the location, the camera and lens, the serial numbers, the owner, the dates and the software are gone, and for a JPEG its thumbnail and comments too;
  • the picture is copied byte for byte: nothing is recompressed, so nothing loses quality;
  • a photo taken sideways keeps its orientation, so it still shows the right way up.

Then press Open the clean copy: hexscope reads the copy back and marks each finding it has checked as removed.

3 Many photos at once

Choose several photos, or drop a folder on the page: each is listed with what it gives away, and Save clean copies saves them all as one ZIP. On a phone, hexscope offers to share the copies instead, so you can save them to your photos or send them straight on.

4 Other ways, and what they leave

  • iPhone: when you share a photo, tap Options at the top and turn off Location. That takes out the location only; the camera and the time stay.
  • Mac: in Preview, Tools → Show Inspector, then the location tab and Remove Location Info. Again, the location only.
  • Windows: right-click a JPEG, Properties → Details → Remove Properties and Personal Information. It works for JPEG files.
  • Exporting from an editor drops some of it, but recompresses the picture, and many editors keep the metadata unless told not to.
  • A screenshot of the photo carries none of the camera's data, but loses detail and adds facts of its own: see What a screenshot gives away.

Whichever way you use, check the result: open the photo you are about to send in hexscope and see what is left.

5 On your own computer

The command line tool cleans a whole folder of photos and reads every copy back to confirm what was removed:

hexscope clean --verify --out clean/ photos/
